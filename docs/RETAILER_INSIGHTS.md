# Retailer insights (owner: Niwin)

Audience: a multi-brand retailer's merchandising/CRM team. Every view shows "Synthetic demo cohort + team data" when synthetic rows are present. Aggregates only; hide any group with fewer than 10 users.

## Questions the dashboard answers
1. **What do customers need next?** Top goals and unmet needs by segment (skin-tone band, region, age band if collected, goal), and how goal demand moves month by month.
2. **Which products actually help which profiles?** Share of users with `real_change` in the target metric within 4/8 weeks, by product x profile (skin-tone band or self-reported skin type) (vs. users with the same goal not using it).
3. **Brand and category performance.** Outcome rate, median time to first real change, continuation rate (still using after 4 weeks and at week 8); per category and goal, how many users reach a real change by week 2, 4 and 8 and how many still use the category's products.
4. **Assortment gaps.** Profiles/goals with low outcome rates or few catalog options in a price tier, with the users per tier.
5. **Routine conflicts.** % of routines with caution-level conflicts, duplicate actives or a missing sunscreen; most common clash pairs (an education + cross-sell opportunity).

## BigQuery (dataset `willow_insights`, us-central1)
Loaded by `api/scripts/export_bq.py` (batch loads, WRITE_TRUNCATE: re-running replaces every table and its schema; creates the dataset if missing; works in sandbox/free tier). `--dry-run` builds and counts without loading, `--today YYYY-MM-DD` fixes the generation date, `--synthetic-only` skips Firestore.
- `sessions_flat`: user_hash, is_synthetic, region, tone_band, skin_type, goals, site, session_ts, weeks_since_baseline, metric_id, delta, mdc95, verdict, improved.
- `users_flat`: one row per person: user_hash, is_synthetic, region, tone_band, skin_type, goals (current), started_at, first_ts, last_ts, last_week.
- `goals_monthly`: one row per person x month with a reading: user_hash, is_synthetic, month (YYYY-MM), last_ts (last reading that month), goals (that month).
- `routine_flat`: user_hash, is_synthetic, product_id, brand, category, concerns, tier, started_week, stopped_week, feel, feel_week, feel_tags (the latest self-reported answer, Oct 17; null when not asked).
- `routine_conflicts`: user_hash, is_synthetic, kind (`retinoid_acid`, `no_spf`, `duplicate_active`, `two_exfoliants`, `bpo_retinoid`, `bpo_vitamin_c`, `other`, or `none`), from the app's rule table (`routine_check.rule_findings`) on the products still in use.
- `products`: catalog copy with goals, target metrics and price tier.

Real rows: people who opted in to aggregate insights (`users/{uid}.consent.aggregate_insights_opt_in`), under a one-way hash, no region. Their goals (the app's 8 ids) and skin type come from `users/{uid}.profile` (opted-in only); only current goals are stored, so every month gets them. Routine start/stop weeks come from `started_at` / `ended_at` against the first reading's baseline.

## Goals
The retailer view groups people by the app's 8 goals (`users/{uid}.profile.goals`, labels as in `web/src/lib/metrics.ts` `GOALS`): Dark spots, Tone evenness, Redness, Blemishes, Pores and texture, Lines, Under-eye, Shine and glow. Catalog concerns map 1:1 (dullness and oiliness both to Shine and glow; dryness has no goal because it is not measured); a test keeps the labels in step with the web. Older rows that hold catalog concerns or the old 7 app ids are mapped on read (`cohort.goals_of`).

## Filters (`GET /v1/retailer/insights?cohort=&range=`)
- `cohort`: `simulated` (synthetic rows only), `real` (opted-in people only), `mixed` (both; default).
- `range`: `30d`, `90d`, `all` (default). A person is in the range when they took a reading in the window; their whole history then counts (an outcome needs the week-8 reading). Month buckets keep only months with a reading in the window. Routine rows have no timestamps, so conflicts follow the people in range.
- Fewer than 10 people in a filter: `empty: true` and only the head count; the page shows "Not enough data in this range" with "Show all time, simulated cohort".
- One BigQuery read is cached 10 min (the last good read is served if BigQuery fails); each filter's aggregates are cached on top of it.

## Synthetic cohort (`api/app/cohort.py`)
- 2,000 recruited people, `is_synthetic=true` on every row; seed fixed for reproducibility. Each takes a baseline on a day in August (45%), September (33%) or October 2026 (22%), then a reading every week up to the export date unless they drop out; people whose first weekly reading is still in the future are not in the data yet (about 1,640 on Oct 15). The random draws do not depend on the date, so a later export only reveals more weeks of the same people.
- Regions mixed (IN, US, UK, KR, SEA, GCC); tone bands spread across all 6 ITA bands; skin type a fixed mix (oily 22%, combination 32%, dry 18%, normal 16%, sensitive 12%); none of them affects which goals and products people pick, and tone band and region do not affect outcomes.
- 1-3 goals each, picked with month-dependent weights (an invented shift to demonstrate the month view: oil and blemishes lead in August; dark spots, tone evenness and redness grow into October). About 1 in 4 people with room add a goal (and a product for it) between weeks 3 and 7.
- Routine of 2-5 catalog products matching goals plus a sunscreen for ~60%; about 3 in 10 product uses stop between weeks 2 and 10; 15% drop out between weeks 2 and 7.
- Switching (Oct 16, invented, to demonstrate the switching views): about half the time a product use stops, the person starts a different catalog product in the same category for the same goal (any product in the category when it has no other one for that goal, as for most sunscreens) 0-2 weeks later (a new routine row with its `started_week`); it has its own effect and about 3 in 10 of these stop again 2-8 weeks later. Switchers lean toward category products that work for others and are popular ("switchers lean toward category products that work for others", invented): the replacement is drawn with weight ((1 + effect size) x (1 + share of people generated so far who use it)) ^ 16 (`cohort.SWITCH_PULL`). The pull is deliberately strong: each stopped product has only about 15 switchers, and a weaker pull spreads them below the 10-person threshold. About 450 switches by Oct 16; 5 of the 8 products with 30 or more people who stopped them show at least one product they were switched to (>= 10 people), and more show a brand flow.
- Per-metric weekly change = product effect (only for metrics mapped to the product's concerns; growing over 6 weeks from when the person starts it; effect size drawn per product from 0.2-1.1 x MDC95, ~30% of products ~0) x a skin-type multiplier + noise drawn from **our measured MDC/SD**; record verdict by comparing |delta| to MDC.
- Skin-type effect multipliers (Oct 16, an invented demonstration assumption so the skin-profile views have something to show, `cohort.SKIN_EFFECT`): oil-control products (oil or blemish concerns, salicylic acid/BHA, zinc, clays, benzoyl peroxide) x1.35 oily, x1.2 combination, x0.8 dry, x0.9 sensitive; barrier and gentle hydrating products (dryness or redness concerns, ceramides, panthenol, centella, squalane) x1.35 dry, x1.3 sensitive, x0.85 oily; strong retinoids and acids (retinol, retinal, glycolic acid, benzoyl peroxide, L-ascorbic acid, 1%+ salicylic acid) x0.55 sensitive, x0.85 dry; traits multiply, plus a random spread of x0.85-1.15 per product and skin type.
- **Self-reported feel (Oct 17).** About 6 in 10 synthetic people answer for a product once it has had two weeks, at a random week up to week 8 of use. "Working" is likelier when a target metric had already improved by then (68%), less likely when it improves later (42%) or not at all (24%), and lower again for products they stopped; early answers lean "too soon to tell". Not-for-me tags follow the product: stings for strong actives, dries for oil-control actives, heavy for moisturisers and sunscreens. Each person x product draws from its own stream (`{seed}:feel:{user}:{product}`), so no other number moves (a test strips feel and checks the Oct 15 digest).
- The new draws use their own random streams (seed + 1, seed + 2): every person, goal, routine, stop and noise draw is the same as before; `cohort.generate(switching=False, skin_effects=False)` reproduces the Oct 15 cohort exactly (a test holds it to that). `export_bq.py --dry-run --synthetic-only --today 2026-10-16`: routine_flat 7,058 rows (was 6,635), routine_conflicts 2,424 (was 2,373); users, sessions and months unchanged.
- The generator's assumptions are on the dashboard's "About this data" panel.

## What the api computes (`api/app/retailer.py`)
Outcome = still improved beyond the noise band at the week-8 reading (a single lucky reading does not count); weeks to change = start of the improved run that holds to week 8. Every group under 10 people is suppressed in the api: cells (`suppressed`), needs-by-month counts (`null`), tier users (`null`), flows and brands (left out, with `brands_hidden` counts), conflicts (left out), median weeks (`null` under 10 changed), tiles (`–`).
- KPIs: users; products with outcomes of the catalog size, with products below 10 people, unused and not measured; per-goal median weeks; continuation at week 8 (people at week 8 with a product for one of their goals still using one).
- Category x goal flow: users at week 8 with the goal using one of the category's products for it; `changed` = how many had a real change (held to week 8) begun by week 2/4/8; `using` = still using one of those products at that week; `base` = people with the goal using none of them (k of n).

## Willow Proof views (Oct 16)
The business product ("Willow Proof", retail intelligence by Willow) reads six more aggregate views, all computed in `api/app/retail_intel.py` from the same BigQuery rows, filters (`cohort`, `range`), cache (one read, then one index per filter, data version and day) and outcome definition as `/v1/retailer/insights`. Public, aggregates only, 503 when the rows are unavailable. Shapes: CONTRACT section 3.

Definitions
- **Eligible person**: reached the week-8 reading. **Outcome**: a lasting change (still improved beyond the person's own noise band at week 8) in any metric the product targets (its concerns' metrics). Goal views use that goal's metrics (narrowed to the ones the product targets).
- **Product users** `n` = eligible people with a routine row for it; `users` = everyone with a row, any week. **Base (non-users)** = eligible people with an overlapping goal and no row for it; `base_rate` only when it has >= 10 people.
- **Rate** CI = Wilson 95%. **Lift** = rate - base_rate, CI = difference +- 1.96 x sqrt(p1 q1 / n1 + p2 q2 / n2), 2 dp.
- **Proof tier**: Proven (n >= 30 and lift_lo > 0), Promising (n >= 10 and lift > 0, not Proven), No clear effect yet (n >= 10, lift <= 0 or no base), Not enough data (n < 10, or `reason` `unused` / `not_measured`: outcome numbers null; `users` and `routine_share` keep their own 10-person rule). `to_proven` (Promising only): people at week 8 still needed for the lift's lower bound to clear zero if both rates hold (at least 30 in all); null when the base group is too small for that.
- **Curve**: for weeks 1-8, the share of users (and of the base) whose lasting change had begun by that week; cumulative, week 8 = rate.
- **Continuation** `cont8`: share of the product's eligible users still using it at week 8. **Routine share**: product users / people using any product of its category (any week).
- **Co-use**: people using both (any week) / the product's users; pairs with >= 10 people, top 6.
- **Switching** A -> B: stopped A at week w and started B, same category, B != A, `started_week` in w-1..w+2. Pairs with >= 10 people; `stopped` = people who stopped A. Product detail also has `to_brands` / `from_brands` (`{brand, label, same_brand, n}`, people counted once per brand, >= 10), which fill better than single products; the product's own brand is labelled "Same brand, other product". Brand view: switches between different brands, `won`/`lost` per brand (>= 10), `net` only when both totals are >= 10.
- **Segments** (`/segments`): per skin type x goal, `users` (people with both), `unmet` (eligible ones with no lasting change in the goal's metrics), `best`/`runner_up` (catalog products for the goal, rate among their eligible users with that skin type on the goal's metrics, n >= 10); per skin type the top 3 goals (share of that skin type), top 5 products by lift against non-users with the same skin type, and `conflict_share` (routines checked with any rule-table finding).
- **Brands**: a brand's `n` = eligible people using one of its measured products, outcome on those products' targets; goal comparison = people with the goal using a product for it, on the goal's metrics, ranked among brands with >= 10 such people; `market` = everyone using a product for the goal.
- **Evidence pack** (product detail): deterministic, claim-ready statements in measured, cosmetic, observational wording ("Measured in 46 Willow users: 78% had a visible change in glow, pores, texture or tone evenness held to week 8 (non-users with the same goals 53%). Simulated cohort."); kinds `efficacy` (overall and the two largest goals), `speed`, `segment` (the skin type with the biggest positive lift), `retention`. Each item carries `method` (standardised phone photos against the person's own baseline and MDC95 noise band; must hold to week 8), `window` ("8 weeks"), `n`, `cohort_label` (Simulated cohort / Consented users / Mixed) and `caveat` ("Observational real-world data, not a controlled trial. Do not present as clinical substantiation. Measured outcomes only; no self-reported results are included."). Never "clinically proven", "proven to treat" or a condition name (UK CAP 3.7 / ASA substantiation, FTC).
- **Eligibility to promote**: tier Proven and at least one goal with its own positive lift (n >= 10); `goals` and `skin_types` = the goals and skin types with a positive lift against non-users (n >= 10 each); `reasons` say why or why not (with the "what would it take" figure for Promising). `retail_intel.eligibility_all()` / `product_eligibility(id)` give the same on cohort=mixed, range=all for campaigns.
- **Competitors** are named (retailer-grade view); every comparison carries `note`: "Observational comparison under the same method; differences inside the confidence intervals are not meaningful."

Suppression: every group under 10 people is null (`suppressed: true` on cells, `n: null`) or left out. **Complementary suppression**: in breakdowns whose total is shown elsewhere (a product's skin-type and tone cells, a brand's skin cells, head counts per skin type), when exactly one cell with people in it is suppressed, the smallest shown cell is suppressed too, so the hidden count cannot be derived as total minus the others. Goal cells overlap (people hold several goals), so they do not get the extra rule. `/v1/retailer/insights` is unchanged (its suppressed cells still carry their small `n`; changing that breaks its contract and tests; to revisit with the web).

Brief (`/brief`, `brand=` optional): up to 6 deterministic cards linking to the exact view. Retailer: top Proven product by lift lower bound (or the Promising product closest to Proven), the biggest skin-type lift, the lowest continuation (n >= 30), the goal whose share of people rose most between the last two months, an assortment gap (a goal with no option in a price tier, else the biggest unmet need), the most common routine conflict. Brand: its best and weakest goal against the market, switching net, its top product, promotable products (or the Promising one closest to Proven), its lowest continuation. Summary: Gemini (same client and model as Ask, temperature 0.2, one attempt, 20 s timeout) writes <= 60 words from the cards (asked for 50); a draft that quotes any number not in the cards, uses banned wording or "clinically", or runs long gets one rewrite told what failed, and if that fails too the summary is null (the cards still show); cached per filter, brand, data version and day (an outage is retried after a minute).

## Proof-gated promotion (Oct 16)
A brand or retailer can pay to show a product inside the consumer app, but only a product whose measured outcomes clear Willow's evidence bar. Money buys reach (one clearly labelled card), never rank or eligibility. Code: `api/app/campaigns.py`, routes `api/app/routes/campaign_routes.py`; contract rows in CONTRACT.md section 3, Firestore `campaigns/**` in section 4.

**Rules (enforced in the api).**
- Eligibility comes from `retail_intel` on cohort=mixed, range=all (the evidence a shopper sees): only Proof tier `proven` (n >= 30 product users and the lift CI above zero). `GET /v1/retailer/eligibility` lists every product with its tier, reasons, eligible goals and the evidence line.
- A campaign's target goals must be among the product's eligible goals, and its skin types among the product's eligible skin types (empty = all eligible skin types, and matching then uses that list; a person without a skin type, or a product with no eligible skin type, never matches). A brand workspace may only promote its own brand ("Retailer (all brands)" may promote any eligible product). The workspace is a demo choice: there is no ownership check of brands yet.
- At most 3 running (active, in date) campaigns per owner (429); 7, 14 or 30 days; owner-only pause, resume and delete. A campaign is `ended` once `ends_at` passes. Resuming re-checks the evidence: a product that slipped below Proven cannot be resumed, and while it is below, no consumer sees its card.
- Rate limits (`app/limits.py`): 20 new campaigns and 60 events per person per day.

**What consumers see.** On Routine, at most one card a day, under the person's own routine: "Sponsored · Willow Proven", the packshot, name, brand, the evidence line with its n and the cohort label ("Willow cohort, includes simulated data"), "View product" (the usual product sheet) and "Hide sponsored". "Why am I seeing this?" shows which of the person's goals (and skin type, when targeted) matched and: "Brands pay to show a product here; only products that meet Willow's evidence bar can be shown, and payment never changes your recommendations. Ranking of your own recommendations is computed from measured outcomes only." Matching: the person's profile goals overlap the campaign's, the skin type is targeted (or, for an untargeted campaign, covered by the evidence), the product is not (and was not) in their routine, and the product is still eligible; among matches the pick rotates deterministically by hash(uid + day). No card with `profile.hide_sponsored`, without goals, in the demo journey, or when the evidence can't be read. You → "Sponsored suggestions" switches it off and on.

**Disclosure.** The word "Sponsored" is the first thing on the card, in text (not a tooltip or colour), FTC-style clear and conspicuous; the disclosure sentence is one tap away on the card itself.

**Organic recommendations never use campaign data.** The advisor, Suggested today, product cards and the retailer analytics read measurements and the catalog only; nothing reads `campaigns/**` except the sponsored card and the campaign owner's own stats.

**Measurement.** Events (`POST /v1/sponsored/{id}/events`): an impression when at least half the card is on screen, a click on "View product", an add when the product is added to the routine from that card. Each person counts once per day for each kind (dedupe key: a hash of uid + day), a click or add only after that day's impression, an add only once the product is really in the routine. Reach is unique people: a person's first counted impression marks `seen` on their hashed membership, once ever. Delivery counts (impressions, clicks, adds, per day) are ad events with no person attribute and are never split by goal or skin type, so they are shown as counted (standard ad reporting: a judge sees "1 impression" right after viewing the card); CTR = clicks / impressions. The holdout comparison appears once each group has 10 people.

**Randomised holdout (incrementality).** Each campaign holds out `holdout_pct` (default 20%) of the people it matches: the first time `GET /v1/sponsored` finds a campaign for a person, they are assigned once and for good by hash(campaign_id + uid) (below the share = holdout) and recorded in `campaigns/{id}/members/{sha256(uid)}` `{group, first_day, added}` (hashed uid only). Holdout members never see that campaign's card (the next matching campaign is tried). When a member adds the product to their routine by any path (search, advisor, card), `added` becomes true. The campaign stats report `incrementality` = adoption rate exposed vs holdout with n per group, lift and its 95% CI (difference of two proportions, as the product lift), `null` until both groups have 10 members. Dashboard note: "Adoption lift vs a randomised holdout. Skin-outcome lift for adopters appears once they reach week 8." Retail-media buyers rank incrementality first, and most retail media reports only exposed-group attribution: this is the measured answer.

## App structure, round 2 (Oct 16): six sections and the Proof pipeline
Supersedes the round-1 layout below where they differ (the endpoints, numbers and suppression rules are unchanged). Look and kit: `docs/RETAIL_BRAND.md`. The shell (`Retail.tsx`) is a Vercel-like top bar (Proof mark / workspace switcher; data scope popover with period and people; "Ask anything ⌘K"; theme: system, light or dark) over a tab row. Every view is in the URL (`?as=brand:<slug>&cohort=&range=` plus each page's own parameters). Old paths redirect: `/retail/promote` to Campaigns, `/segments` and `/demand` to Customers, `/competitors` and `/categories/:c` to Market; brief cards link to the new places (`ui/insight.ts` `briefHref`; demand cards use the anchor alone, e.g. `/retail/customers?goal=even_tone#unmet`).

The demo story is one product's **Proof pipeline** (`features/retail/pipeline.tsx`): Measured → Proven → Claims ready → Promoted → Lift measured, each step a fact from the api.

- **Overview** (`/retail`): the header sentence gives the Proven count. The **spotlight** is the strongest Proven product (yours in a brand workspace; ranked by the lower end of its lift interval, else the best Promising one): users vs non-users with the lift, its outcome curve, and the pipeline beside it with View proof and Promote. Then four KPIs (brand: Proven products, visible change vs the market, still using at week 8, people, the brand's own figures from `/brands` so they match Market; retailer: Proven, visible change, no visible change yet (people at week 8 with a goal still without one), people measured), the brief (Gemini summary as the panel's description, cards as rows that link to their view) and your top products. About (method, consent, Data Studio) is collapsed at the bottom.
- **Products** (`/retail/products`): status tabs with counts (All, Proven, Promising, No clear effect, Not enough data; `tier=`), one table with search, brand or "Your products / All products" (`scope=all`), category and goal filters, a Columns menu (Category, Weeks to change, Still using wk 8, Share of routines, Price tier are optional) and CSV. Default sort is lift. Tick up to 3 rows for Compare. A row opens the product page.
- **Product page** (`/retail/products/:id`): breadcrumb, header (packshot, brand, facts, status and rank) with Compare, Export (evidence pack as HTML, Markdown or CSV) and **Promote** (primary; disabled with the reason until the product is eligible). Then the pipeline, the outcome (hero rate, non-users, lift, curve, four stats) beside "Where it works" (skin type / goal / tone tabs), claim-ready statements (copy each, method and caveat in the footnote), against other products in its category, and routines (used alongside, where users go, conflicts; `ctx=`).
- **Compare** (`/retail/compare?ids=`): up to three products side by side, the leader of each row in semibold, curves overlaid, skin types × products.
- **Customers** (`/retail/customers`): the header names the most under-served group. A skin type × goal matrix (`show=` unmet need, people or best product; your leading groups outlined in a brand workspace; `cell=` opens the group's best products and yours), then skin types (`skin=` side panel), what people want with monthly trend (`#demand`), routine conflicts (`#conflicts`) and assortment gaps (`#gaps`).
- **Market** (`/retail/market`): **Brands** (brand workspace: you vs the market per goal with the best competitor, positioning, switching `#switching`, share of category routines, skin types; retailer workspace: the brand leaderboard and positioning, `brand=` opens a brand panel with "View as") and **Categories** (`view=categories&category=`: visible change by goal at weeks 2/4/8 against non-users, brands in the category, top products).
- **Campaigns** (`/retail/campaigns`): proof-gated promotion (rules as in "Proof-gated promotion" above, stated once as a panel footer). The header sentence gives running campaigns, people reached and the pooled lift over the holdout. A campaigns table (product, status with days left, audience, reach in people, adds, incremental lift or "Collecting · needs 10 per group"; impressions, clicks, CTR, holdout and dates optional; `?all=1` adds other workspaces) whose rows open a side panel (`?campaign=<id>`: lift with exposed vs holdout adoption, n and 95% interval, delivery (reach, impressions, clicks, CTR, adds) with daily bars, Pause/Resume, End and Delete). Then "Ready to promote" (eligible products with Promote; "Show not eligible yet" lists the api's reasons). `?new=<product_id>` (from Promote anywhere) opens the create sheet prefilled: product, audience (only the goals and skin types the evidence covers, narrow-only, with a matching-profiles range from the cohort), 7/14/30 days, holdout 10-50% (default 20%), and the exact Sponsored card with "Why am I seeing this?". Reach is unique people shown the card (each person once); impressions count each person once a day. "End campaign…" stops a campaign now and for good (it cannot be resumed; its results stay; one that ended before both groups had 10 people reads "Not measured", and the pipeline's Promoted step then offers "Promote again"); Delete removes it and its stats. The header's "people reached" sums each campaign's people, so a person two campaigns reached counts in both.
- **Explore** (`/retail/explore`): a query builder over the product rows: measure (visible change rate, lift vs non-users, people measured, still using at week 8, weeks to first change, share of routines), group by (product, brand, category, price tier, Proof status, goal), filters, minimum people, sort and limit, five presets; a scale-true bar chart (your brand in leaf, intervals, non-user ticks) and a table, with Copy link and CSV. Groups are people-weighted (Σ value·n / Σ n; lift = weighted rate minus weighted non-user rate); groups with nothing left are dropped, never shown as 0.
