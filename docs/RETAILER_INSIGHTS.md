# Retailer insights (owner: Niwin)

Audience: a multi-brand retailer's merchandising/CRM team. Every view shows "Synthetic demo cohort + team data" when synthetic rows are present. Aggregates only; hide any group with fewer than 10 users.

## Questions the dashboard answers
1. **What do customers need next?** Top goals and unmet needs by skin type and goal, and how goal demand moves month by month (region and age band are not shown).
2. **Which products actually help which profiles?** Share of users with `real_change` in the target metric within 4/8 weeks, by product x profile (skin-tone band or self-reported skin type) (vs. users with the same goal not using it).
3. **Brand and category performance.** Outcome rate, median time to first real change, continuation rate (still using after 4 weeks and at week 8); per category and goal, how many users reach a real change by week 2, 4 and 8 and how many still use the category's products.
4. **Assortment gaps.** Profiles/goals with low outcome rates or few catalog options in a price tier, with the users per tier.
5. **Routine conflicts.** % of routines with caution-level conflicts, duplicate actives or a missing sunscreen; most common clash pairs (an education + cross-sell opportunity).

## BigQuery (dataset `willow_insights`, us-central1)
Loaded by `api/scripts/export_bq.py` (batch loads, WRITE_TRUNCATE: re-running replaces every table and its schema; creates the dataset if missing; works in sandbox/free tier). `--dry-run` builds and counts without loading, `--today YYYY-MM-DD` fixes the generation date, `--synthetic-only` skips Firestore.
- `sessions_flat`: user_hash, is_synthetic, region, tone_band, skin_type, goals, site, session_ts, weeks_since_baseline, metric_id, delta, mdc95, verdict, improved.
- `users_flat`: one row per person: user_hash, is_synthetic, region, tone_band, skin_type, goals (current), started_at, first_ts, last_ts, last_week.
- `goals_monthly`: one row per person x month with a reading: user_hash, is_synthetic, month (YYYY-MM), last_ts (last reading that month), goals (that month).
- `routine_flat`: user_hash, is_synthetic, product_id, brand, category, concerns, tier, started_week, stopped_week, feel, feel_week, feel_tags (the latest self-reported answer, Oct 17; null when not asked).
- `routine_conflicts`: user_hash, is_synthetic, kind (`retinoid_acid`, `no_spf`, `duplicate_active`, `two_exfoliants`, `bpo_retinoid`, `bpo_vitamin_c`, `other`, or `none`), from the app's rule table (`routine_check.rule_findings`) on the products still in use.
- `products`: catalog copy with goals, target metrics and price tier.

Real rows: people who opted in to aggregate insights (`users/{uid}.consent.aggregate_insights_opt_in`), under a one-way hash, no region. Their goals (the app's 8 ids) and skin type come from `users/{uid}.profile` (opted-in only); only current goals are stored, so every month gets them. Routine start/stop weeks come from `started_at` / `ended_at` against the first reading's baseline.

## Goals
The retailer view groups people by the app's 8 goals (`users/{uid}.profile.goals`, labels as in `web/src/lib/metrics.ts` `GOALS`): Dark spots, Tone evenness, Redness, Blemishes, Pores and texture, Lines, Under-eye, Shine and glow. Catalog concerns map 1:1 (dullness and oiliness both to Shine and glow; dryness has no goal because it is not measured); a test keeps the labels in step with the web. Older rows that hold catalog concerns or the old 7 app ids are mapped on read (`cohort.goals_of`).

## Filters (`GET /v1/retailer/insights?cohort=&range=`)
- `cohort`: `simulated` (synthetic rows only), `real` (opted-in people only), `mixed` (both; default).
- `range`: `30d`, `90d`, `all` (default). A person is in the range when they took a reading in the window; their whole history then counts (an outcome needs the week-8 reading). Month buckets keep only months with a reading in the window. Routine rows have no timestamps, so conflicts follow the people in range.
- Fewer than 10 people in a filter: `empty: true` and only the head count; the page shows "Not enough data in this range" with "Show all time, simulated cohort".
- One BigQuery read is cached 10 min (the last good read is served if BigQuery fails); each filter's aggregates are cached on top of it.

## Synthetic cohort (`api/app/cohort.py`)
- 35,000 simulated people (`cohort.USERS`; 2,000 until Nov 28), `is_synthetic=true` on every row; seed fixed for reproducibility. Each takes a baseline on a day in August (45%), September (33%) or October 2026 (22%), then a reading every week up to the export date unless they drop out; people whose first weekly reading is still in the future are not in the data yet. The random draws do not depend on the date, so a later export only reveals more weeks of the same people.
- Products (Nov 28): the 1,500 best-known products in `api/seed/cohort_products.json`, picked by `api/scripts/pick_cohort_products.py` (deterministic): the 48 of the first cohort in order, every full product with a measured concern, then index products of famous brands from discovery (`research/finals/catalog-brands.json`) by hero-product rank (round robin across brands), with quotas per category (serums 25%, moisturisers 17%, cleansers 14%, sunscreens 13%, ...) and per region (GLOBAL, SEA, IN, AU, US/UK/EU). Index products have no researched concerns, so what Proof measures them on is inferred from the name and category into `api/seed/cohort_concerns.json` (1,285 products) and read only by the cohort and Proof (`cohort.concerns_of`); the consumer catalog is unchanged. The catalog tags no product JP or CN and 19 KR, so people there use GLOBAL products (mostly K- and J-beauty brands) first.
- Regions (Nov 28): a mix for a Singapore-based retailer (SEA 34%, IN 12%, AU 10%, KR 8%, JP 7%, CN 7%, US 7%, GCC 6%, UK 5%, EU 4%). Routines lean to products sold near the person: pick weight by distance (`app/regions.py` order: own region x3, GLOBAL x2.2, next x1.4, x1.2, x1.0, farther x0.8) times a mild fame weight (1 for the best known product down to about 0.73). Tone bands spread across all 6 ITA bands; skin type a fixed mix (oily 22%, combination 32%, dry 18%, normal 16%, sensitive 12%); none of them affects which goals people pick, and tone band and region do not affect outcomes.
- 1-3 goals each, picked with month-dependent weights (an invented shift to demonstrate the month view: oil and blemishes lead in August; dark spots, tone evenness and redness grow into October). About 1 in 4 people with room add a goal (and a product for it) between weeks 3 and 7.
- Routine of 2-5 catalog products matching goals plus a sunscreen for ~60%; about 3 in 10 product uses stop between weeks 2 and 10; 15% drop out between weeks 2 and 7.
- Switching (Oct 16, invented, to demonstrate the switching views): about half the time a product use stops, the person starts a different catalog product in the same category for the same goal (any product in the category when it has no other one for that goal, as for most sunscreens) 0-2 weeks later (a new routine row with its `started_week`); it has its own effect and about 3 in 10 of these stop again 2-8 weeks later. Switchers lean toward category products that work for others and are popular ("switchers lean toward category products that work for others", invented): the replacement is drawn with weight ((1 + effect size) x (1 + share of people generated so far who use it)) ^ 16 (`cohort.SWITCH_PULL`). The pull is deliberately strong: each stopped product has only about 15 switchers, and a weaker pull spreads them below the 10-person threshold. About 450 switches by Oct 16; 5 of the 8 products with 30 or more people who stopped them show at least one product they were switched to (>= 10 people), and more show a brand flow.
- Per-metric weekly change = product effect (only for metrics mapped to the product's concerns; growing over 6 weeks from when the person starts it; effect size drawn per product from 0.45-1.2 x MDC95 (0.2-1.1 until Nov 28), ~30% of products 0) x a skin-type multiplier + noise drawn from **our measured MDC/SD**; record verdict by comparing |delta| to MDC.
- Skin-type effect multipliers (Oct 16, an invented demonstration assumption so the skin-profile views have something to show, `cohort.SKIN_EFFECT`): oil-control products (oil or blemish concerns, salicylic acid/BHA, zinc, clays, benzoyl peroxide) x1.35 oily, x1.2 combination, x0.8 dry, x0.9 sensitive; barrier and gentle hydrating products (dryness or redness concerns, ceramides, panthenol, centella, squalane) x1.35 dry, x1.3 sensitive, x0.85 oily; strong retinoids and acids (retinol, retinal, glycolic acid, benzoyl peroxide, L-ascorbic acid, 1%+ salicylic acid) x0.55 sensitive, x0.85 dry; traits multiply, plus a random spread of x0.85-1.15 per product and skin type.
- **Self-reported feel (Oct 17).** About 6 in 10 synthetic people answer for a product once it has had two weeks, at a random week up to week 8 of use. "Working" is likelier when a target metric had already improved by then (68%), less likely when it improves later (42%) or not at all (24%), and lower again for products they stopped; early answers lean "too soon to tell". Not-for-me tags follow the product: stings for strong actives, dries for oil-control actives, heavy for moisturisers and sunscreens. Each person x product draws from its own stream (`{seed}:feel:{user}:{product}`), so no other number moves (a test strips feel and checks the Oct 15 digest).
- The new draws use their own random streams (seed + 1, seed + 2): every person, goal, routine, stop and noise draw is the same as before; `cohort.generate(switching=False, skin_effects=False)` reproduces the cohort without those two assumptions draw for draw (a test holds it to a digest; it was the Oct 15 cohort until the Nov 28 scale-up re-baselined it). `export_bq.py --dry-run --synthetic-only --today 2026-10-16`: routine_flat 7,058 rows (was 6,635), routine_conflicts 2,424 (was 2,373); users, sessions and months unchanged.
- Scale (Nov 28; `export_bq.py --dry-run --synthetic-only`): on Nov 28 sessions_flat 3.84 M rows, routine_flat 158 k, users 35 k, months 98 k (4.17 M in all); on Dec 4 4.47 M in all (sessions 4.12 M, about 1.4 GB as NDJSON). Generation takes 13-15 s and about 1.8 GB. Every reading keeps all 10 metrics (under the 5 M rows where per-person rows would be trimmed). Proof tiers among the 1,500 (cohort=mixed, range=all): Nov 28 Proven 643, Promising 285, No clear effect 567, Not enough data 5; Dec 4 Proven 657, Promising 277, No clear effect 561, Not enough data 5. No clear effect is mostly the 3 in 10 products with no effect; Promising is mostly products in AU and US/UK/EU with fewer users. Tests run 2,000 people over the first 48 products (`tests/conftest.py`), so the Oct 15 digest was re-baselined.
- The generator's assumptions are on the dashboard's "About this data" panel.
- **Sample campaigns (Nov 28, `api/app/dev_seed.py`).** A demo seat's first "Try as" seeds a portfolio built from the measured outcomes: a retailer org gets 11 campaigns on the best-known Proven products sold in SEA or GLOBAL (one per brand, at most two per category; slotted by measured lift: 2 finished with a clear lift, 1 finished whose interval includes zero, 1 stopped early, 4 running, 2 collecting with a holdout under 10, 1 paused); a brand org 3-5 of its own Proven products (none if it has none). Reach 400-3,200 people (collecting 24-40), CTR 20-30%, holdout 20%, adoption over the holdout 4-14 points, more for a larger measured lift. Group counts are stored on the campaign (`tally`, read by `campaigns.stats`) instead of one document per person: about 200 Firestore writes per retailer seat.

## What the api computes (`api/app/retailer.py`)
Outcome = still improved beyond the noise band at the week-8 reading (a single lucky reading does not count); weeks to change = start of the improved run that holds to week 8. Every group under 10 people is suppressed in the api: cells (`suppressed`), needs-by-month counts (`null`), tier users (`null`), flows and brands (left out, with `brands_hidden` counts), conflicts (left out), median weeks (`null` under 10 changed), tiles (`–`).
- KPIs: users; products with outcomes of the catalog size, with products below 10 people, unused and not measured; per-goal median weeks; continuation at week 8 (people at week 8 with a product for one of their goals still using one).
- Category x goal flow: users at week 8 with the goal using one of the category's products for it; `changed` = how many had a real change (held to week 8) begun by week 2/4/8; `using` = still using one of those products at that week; `base` = people with the goal using none of them (k of n).

## Willow Proof views (Oct 16)
The business product ("Proof by Willow", formerly "Willow Proof") reads six more aggregate views, all computed in `api/app/retail_intel.py` from the same BigQuery rows, filters (`cohort`, `range`), cache (one read, then one index per filter, data version and day) and outcome definition as `/v1/retailer/insights`. Public, aggregates only, 503 when the rows are unavailable. Shapes: CONTRACT section 3.

Definitions
- **Eligible person**: reached the week-8 reading. **Outcome**: a lasting change (still improved beyond the person's own noise band at week 8) in any metric the product targets (its concerns' metrics). Goal views use that goal's metrics (narrowed to the ones the product targets).
- **Product users** `n` = eligible people with a routine row for it; `users` = everyone with a row, any week. **Base (non-users)** = eligible people with an overlapping goal and no row for it; `base_rate` only when it has >= 10 people.
- **Rate** CI = Wilson 95%. **Lift** = rate - base_rate, CI = difference +- 1.96 x sqrt(p1 q1 / n1 + p2 q2 / n2), 2 dp.
- **Proof tier**: Proven (n >= 30 and lift_lo > 0), Promising (n >= 10 and lift > 0, not Proven), No clear effect yet (n >= 10, lift <= 0 or no base), Not enough data (n < 10, or `reason` `unused` / `not_measured`: outcome numbers null; `users` and `routine_share` keep their own 10-person rule). `to_proven` (Promising only): people at week 8 still needed for the lift's lower bound to clear zero if both rates hold (at least 30 in all); null when the base group is too small for that.
- **Curve**: for weeks 1-8, the share of users (and of the base) whose lasting change had begun by that week; cumulative, week 8 = rate.
- **Continuation** `cont8`: share of the product's eligible users still using it at week 8. **Routine share**: product users / people using any product of its category (any week).
- **Co-use**: people using both (any week) / the product's users; pairs with >= 10 people, top 6.
- **Switching** A -> B: stopped A at week w and started B, same category, B != A, `started_week` in w-1..w+2. Pairs with >= 10 people; `stopped` = people who stopped A. Product detail also has `to_brands` / `from_brands` (`{brand, label, same_brand, n}`, people counted once per brand, >= 10), which fill better than single products; the product's own brand is labelled "Same brand, other product". Brand view: switches between different brands, `won`/`lost` per brand (>= 10), `net` only when both totals are >= 10.
- **Segments** (`/segments`): per skin type x goal, `users` (people with both), `unmet` (eligible ones with no lasting change in the goal's metrics), `best`/`runner_up` (catalog products for the goal, rate among their eligible users with that skin type on the goal's metrics, n >= 10); per skin type the top 3 goals (share of that skin type), top 5 products by lift against non-users with the same skin type, and `conflict_share` (routines checked with any rule-table finding).
- **Brands**: a brand's `n` = eligible people using one of its measured products, outcome on those products' targets; goal comparison = people with the goal using a product for it, on the goal's metrics, ranked among brands with >= 10 such people; `market` = everyone using a product for the goal.
- **Evidence pack** (product detail): deterministic, claim-ready statements in measured, cosmetic, observational wording ("Measured in 46 Willow users: 78% had a visible change in glow, pores, texture or tone evenness held to week 8 (non-users with the same goals 53%). Simulated cohort."); kinds `efficacy` (overall and the two largest goals), `speed`, `segment` (the skin type with the biggest positive lift), `retention`. Each item carries `method` (standardised phone photos against the person's own baseline and MDC95 noise band; must hold to week 8), `window` ("8 weeks"), `n`, `cohort_label` (Simulated cohort / Consented users / Mixed) and `caveat` ("Observational real-world data, not a controlled trial. Do not present as clinical substantiation. Measured outcomes only; no self-reported results are included."). Never "clinically proven", "proven to treat" or a condition name (UK CAP 3.7 / ASA substantiation, FTC).
- **Eligibility to promote**: tier Proven and at least one goal with its own positive lift (n >= 10); `goals` and `skin_types` = the goals and skin types with a positive lift against non-users (n >= 10 each); `reasons` say why or why not (with the "what would it take" figure for Promising). `retail_intel.eligibility_all()` / `product_eligibility(id)` give the same on cohort=mixed, range=all for campaigns.
- **Competitors** are named (retailer-grade view); every comparison carries `note`: "Observational comparison under the same method; differences inside the confidence intervals are not meaningful."

Suppression: every group under 10 people is null (`suppressed: true` on cells, `n: null`) or left out. **Complementary suppression**: in breakdowns whose total is shown elsewhere (a product's skin-type and tone cells, a brand's skin cells, head counts per skin type), when exactly one cell with people in it is suppressed, the smallest shown cell is suppressed too, so the hidden count cannot be derived as total minus the others. Goal cells overlap (people hold several goals), so they do not get the extra rule. `/v1/retailer/insights` is unchanged (its suppressed cells still carry their small `n`; changing that breaks its contract and tests; to revisit with the web).

Brief (`/brief`, `brand=` optional): up to 6 deterministic cards linking to the exact view. Retailer: top Proven product by lift lower bound (or the Promising product closest to Proven), the biggest skin-type lift, the lowest continuation (n >= 30), the goal whose share of people rose most between the last two months, an assortment gap (a goal with no option in a price tier, else the biggest unmet need), the most common routine conflict. Brand: its best and weakest goal against the market, switching net, its top product, promotable products (or the Promising one closest to Proven), its lowest continuation. Summary: Gemini (same client and model as Ask, temperature 0.2, one attempt, 20 s timeout) writes <= 60 words from the cards (asked for 50); a draft that quotes any number not in the cards, uses banned wording or "clinically", or runs long gets one rewrite told what failed, and if that fails too the summary is null (the cards still show); cached per filter, brand, data version and day (an outage is retried after a minute).

**Journey** (`/products/{id}` field `journey`, added Oct 18; to be written into CONTRACT section 3 once both owners agree): a tree of real counts `{weeks, nodes: [{id, col, label, lane: users|non, n, more}], links: [{source, target, n}]}` for the product's chart "From trying it to how it feels". Users -> week 8 reading (reached / not yet) -> outcome (lasting change or not) -> still using at week 8 -> how it feels (self-reported, week 2 or later). Non-users with the same goals are one node (`non`, lane `non`) with no children: their outcome is the comparison in the product's figures, not a flow. Every group splits once, suppression runs per parent (complementary rule included), and a node with `more: true` has a part under 10 people left out. Null when the tier is Not enough data (`too_few`). Repeat purchase is not tracked, so the tree ends at continuation.

## Proof-gated promotion (Oct 16)
A brand or retailer can pay to show a product inside the consumer app, but only a product whose measured outcomes clear Willow's evidence bar. Money buys reach (one clearly labelled card), never rank or eligibility. Code: `api/app/campaigns.py`, routes `api/app/routes/campaign_routes.py`; contract rows in CONTRACT.md section 3, Firestore `campaigns/**` in section 4.

**Rules (enforced in the api).**
- Eligibility comes from `retail_intel` on cohort=mixed, range=all (the evidence a shopper sees): only Proof tier `proven` (n >= 30 product users and the lift CI above zero). `GET /v1/retailer/eligibility` lists every product with its tier, reasons, eligible goals and the evidence line.
- A campaign's target goals must be among the product's eligible goals, and its skin types among the product's eligible skin types (empty = all eligible skin types, and matching then uses that list; a person without a skin type, or a product with no eligible skin type, never matches). Creating a campaign needs an analyst or admin membership of the retailer organisation, or of the brand organisation that owns the product's brand (a brand promotes only its own brands); campaigns by demo members reach guest accounts only ("Who is looking" below).
- At most 3 running (active, in date) campaigns per owner (429); 7, 14 or 30 days; owner-only pause, resume and delete. A campaign is `ended` once `ends_at` passes. Resuming re-checks the evidence: a product that slipped below Proven cannot be resumed, and while it is below, no consumer sees its card.
- Rate limits (`app/limits.py`): 20 new campaigns and 60 events per person per day.

**What consumers see.** On Routine, at most one card a day, under the person's own routine: "Sponsored · Willow Proven", the packshot, name, brand, the evidence line with its n and the cohort label ("Willow cohort, includes simulated data"), "View product" (the usual product sheet) and "Hide sponsored". "Why am I seeing this?" shows which of the person's goals (and skin type, when targeted) matched and: "Brands pay to show a product here; only products that meet Willow's evidence bar can be shown, and payment never changes your recommendations. Ranking of your own recommendations is computed from measured outcomes only." Matching: the person's profile goals overlap the campaign's, the skin type is targeted (or, for an untargeted campaign, covered by the evidence), the product is not (and was not) in their routine, and the product is still eligible; among matches the pick rotates deterministically by hash(uid + day). No card with `profile.hide_sponsored`, without goals, in the demo journey, or when the evidence can't be read. You → "Sponsored suggestions" switches it off and on.

**Disclosure.** The word "Sponsored" is the first thing on the card, in text (not a tooltip or colour), FTC-style clear and conspicuous; the disclosure sentence is one tap away on the card itself.

**Organic recommendations never use campaign data.** The advisor, Suggested today, product cards and the retailer analytics read measurements and the catalog only; nothing reads `campaigns/**` except the sponsored card and the campaign owner's own stats.

**Measurement.** Events (`POST /v1/sponsored/{id}/events`): an impression when at least half the card is on screen, a click on "View product", an add when the product is added to the routine from that card. Each person counts once per day for each kind (dedupe key: a hash of uid + day), a click or add only after that day's impression, an add only once the product is really in the routine. Reach is unique people: a person's first counted impression marks `seen` on their hashed membership, once ever. Delivery counts (impressions, clicks, adds, per day) are ad events with no person attribute and are never split by goal or skin type, so they are shown as counted (standard ad reporting: a judge sees "1 impression" right after viewing the card); CTR = clicks / impressions. The holdout comparison appears once each group has 10 people.

**Randomised holdout (incrementality).** Each campaign holds out `holdout_pct` (default 20%) of the people it matches: the first time `GET /v1/sponsored` finds a campaign for a person, they are assigned once and for good by hash(campaign_id + uid) (below the share = holdout) and recorded in `campaigns/{id}/members/{sha256(uid)}` `{group, first_day, added}` (hashed uid only). Holdout members never see that campaign's card (the next matching campaign is tried). When a member adds the product to their routine by any path (search, advisor, card), `added` becomes true. The campaign stats report `incrementality` = adoption rate exposed vs holdout with n per group, lift and its 95% CI (difference of two proportions, as the product lift), `null` until both groups have 10 members. Dashboard note: "Adoption lift vs a randomised holdout. Skin-outcome lift for adopters appears once they reach week 8." Retail-media buyers rank incrementality first, and most retail media reports only exposed-group attribution: this is the measured answer.

## App structure (finals, Nov 27)
Look, kit and nav: `docs/RETAIL_BRAND.md`. Every view is in the URL (`?instance=&cohort=&range=` plus each page's own keys; a retailer's brand lens is `?as=brand:<slug>`). Old round-1 paths redirect (`/retail/promote` → Campaigns, `/segments` and `/demand` → Customers, `/competitors` and `/categories/:c` → Market); the round-1 layout itself is retired.

**Who is looking: instances, organisations, roles** (api `proof_orgs.py`, `routes/proof_routes.py`; web `org.tsx`). An instance is one retailer's Proof deployment (`proof.northside.beauty`, resolved from the hostname; unknown hosts get the `willow` instance with one brand organisation per catalog brand). Inside it: the retailer organisation (every brand in its range) and brand organisations (own brand names, "Verified from Northside's supplier list"). Sign-in is required (Google or guest); members have a role: admin (team, data-sharing policy, campaigns), analyst (decide, draft, send, upload the range, export), viewer (read). On a demo instance anyone can "Try as" any organisation ("Choose how to explore"; `?choose=1` reopens it, e.g. from AutoCampaign's "needs an analyst" note); those memberships and their campaigns are `demo` and reach guest accounts only. A brand organisation is locked to its own brand.

**Navigation**: Home · Products (Products | Your range) · Customers · Market · Campaigns (Campaigns | AutoCampaign | Shelf check) · Research · Settings, and "Advanced" (Explore, Compare, the Overview board). Each page header carries one line: the decision it supports and what you gain.

- **Home = Decisions** (`/retail`, `sections/Home.tsx`, cards composed in the browser by `decisions.ts` from data Proof already serves, inputs read once by `homeData.ts`). Header "N to decide · N in progress · N results"; a four-figure strip (range, measured, Proven, campaigns); a first-run "How Proof works" checklist that ticks from real state. Three lanes: **To decide** (at most five, most people first; each card has its trigger sentence, the figure with n and interval, one primary and one secondary action, Snooze for a week and Dismiss with a reason), **In progress** (accepted cards naming the measurement they wait for, live campaigns, open evidence requests) and **Results** (campaign results with their interval, brands' answers, done decisions). Card state lives on the organisation (`PUT /v1/proof/orgs/{org}/decisions/{key}`, key `<kind>--<id>`), every change is logged with the numbers at the time (Decision log). Insight types (research 06 §6.5): 1 Proven, not promoted; 2 Nearly proven (sampling brief); 3 Claim ready; 4 Evidence slipped (a live campaign on a product no longer Proven, or a saved claim on one below Promising; pause, or withdraw/reword); 5 Expectation gap (brand: at least 25% of 10+ people at week 8 changed but didn't say it's working; "expectation line" brief with the median week); 6 Over-shelved, under-delivering and 7 Under-shelved performer (from the Shelf check's own flags); 8 Unmet group (sourcing brief); 9 Demand shift (a goal's share up 3+ points month on month); 10 Losing switchers (brand: net switching negative and 10+ people to one destination; retention note; the destination named by policy); 11 Routine clash (a rule-table conflict in 20%+ of routines: a brand's own products from their pages, the retailer across all routines checked; layering note); 12 Campaign result in; plus AutoCampaign proposals as ordinary cards. Retailer mix: 4, 6-9, 11, 12, proposals; brand mix: 1-5, 10, 11, 12, proposals. A type appears only where its data exists. Evidence requests (Inbox / Sent) sit below the lanes.
- **Weekly digest preview** (`/retail/digest`, Home → "Weekly digest"; `digest.ts`): what the organisation's admins and analysts would receive each Monday: decisions waiting, results in, evidence requests and the range's coverage since the item file was connected (statuses at upload vs today). Marked "Preview · not sent": sending needs an email provider; the production path is a weekly job rendering the same view. Downloadable as Markdown.
- **Range** (`/retail/range`, `range.ts`, `rangeStore.ts`): "Connect your range" in three steps. Source: the retailer's item file as CSV (gtin, product_id or brand + product, category, price, facings; a template is generated from the catalog). Match: by Willow id, else brand + name (GTIN kept, not matched: the catalog has no barcodes) → Measured / Collecting / In range, no users yet / Not measured by Willow / Not recognised. Coverage: one sentence with the counts. The matched rows are **saved on the organisation** (`GET/PUT/DELETE /v1/proof/orgs/{org}/range`, Firestore `proof_ranges/{org}`, up to 500 rows; analyst+ of the retailer writes; the api drops any match that isn't a catalog product). A brand organisation reads the retailer's file filtered to its own rows (`from` names the retailer) and cannot write one; until the retailer connects a file it sees its catalog products, said as such. The browser keeps a copy only when the range endpoint fails. Unrecognised rows go to the product-request queue; facings also feed the Shelf check. Products, Home's figures and Research's packs default to the range.
- **Research** (`/retail/research`, `GET /v1/proof/research`): method, cohort facts, metric dictionary, claim rules, data sharing, glossary and evidence packs, all read from the api's code; a downloadable Methods appendix.
- **Settings** (`/retail/settings`): organisation profile, team (members, invite → pending row; no email sent), data sharing (minimum group 10, fixed; the competitor-names switch for the retailer's admin), plan.
- **Product page** (`/retail/products/:id`): breadcrumb, header (packshot, brand, facts, status and rank) with Compare, Export (evidence pack as HTML, Markdown or CSV) and **Promote** (primary; disabled with the reason until the product is eligible). Then the pipeline, the outcome (hero rate, non-users, lift, curve, four stats) beside "Where it works" (skin type / goal / tone tabs), claim-ready statements (copy each, method and caveat in the footnote), against other products in its category, and routines (used alongside, where users go, conflicts; `ctx=`). Between the evidence and the routines sits **How it feels** (`#feel`, `sections/FeelPanel.tsx`, helpers `feel.ts`, tests `tests/feel.test.ts`), labelled "Self-reported" once, with the api's `feel.note` as its footnote and never mixed into the tier, the evidence pack or a claim: the three answers as one stacked bar (leaf = "It's working for me"; the legend gives each whole percent and n, and is the bar's text alternative), the top tags as "Liked" and "Not liked" (share and n each), and **Felt vs measured** (`agree`) as a 2×2 (rows: visible change or not; columns: said it's working or not) with one sentence per cell; measured-only reads "Changed but didn't feel it yet: the people most likely to stop early. Set expectations on timing." `agree` null: one line saying the product isn't measured, or fewer than 10 people at week 8 have answered. `feel` null: "Fewer than 10 people have said how it feels yet." The brief's `feel_gap` card (kind risk) links to `/retail/products/<id>#feel`.
  - **Next steps** (`sections/NextSteps.tsx`, helpers `proofAi.ts`, tests `tests/proof-ai.test.ts`) sits under the pipeline on `GET /v1/retailer/products/{id}/actions`: up to three rows (title, the api's `why`, one action). `campaigns` opens the create sheet prefilled (`/retail/campaigns?new=<id>&skins=a,b`); the others scroll to their panel and pick its tab (`seg=`, `ctx=`) or claim channel (`claim=`); a claims step on a product that isn't Proven or Promising goes to Felt vs measured instead. Provenance line "Picked by Gemini from N things the data supports" or "From the data". Skeleton rows while loading; hidden on error or no steps.
  - **Claim drafts** (`sections/ClaimStudio.tsx`) under the statements: channel picker (Pack, Product page, Retailer listing, Social; `claim=`), "Draft claims" calls `POST .../claims` (sign-in, never on page load; after the first run a new channel drafts again). Each draft: text, source chips (short label; hover shows, tap pins the statement; "Self-reported" outlined), Copy. The caveat and the checks line in the footnote; `blocked` (or a tier the api won't draft for) is the empty state with a link to Next steps.
- **Compare** (`/retail/compare?ids=`): up to three products side by side, the leader of each row in semibold, curves overlaid, skin types × products.
- **Customers** (`/retail/customers`): the header names the most under-served group. A skin type × goal matrix (`show=` unmet need, people or best product; your leading groups outlined in a brand workspace; `cell=` opens the group's best products and yours), then skin types (`skin=` side panel), what people want with monthly trend (`#demand`), routine conflicts (`#conflicts`) and assortment gaps (`#gaps`). **Fit and reactions** (`#fit`, `sections/customers/Fit.tsx`) closes the page: ingredient groups opted-in shoppers avoid, products they put on their avoid list or reported a reaction to (`GET /v1/proof/orgs/{org}/fit`, reading `users/{uid}.profile.safety.avoid` / `avoid_products` and `users/{uid}.reactions` only for people with `consent.aggregate_insights_opt_in`; counts of people, any group under 10 left out; a brand sees its own products), and store taps in the last 30 days (`GET /v1/retailer/clicks`). The simulated cohort has no safety profiles, so with little real data the panel says so.
- **Market** (`/retail/market`): **Brands** (brand workspace: you vs the market per goal with the best competitor, positioning, switching `#switching`, share of category routines, skin types; retailer workspace: the brand leaderboard and positioning, `brand=` opens a brand panel with "View as") and **Categories** (`view=categories&category=`: visible change by goal at weeks 2/4/8 against non-users, brands in the category, top products).
- **Campaigns** (`/retail/campaigns`): proof-gated promotion (rules as in "Proof-gated promotion" above, stated once as a panel footer). The header sentence gives running campaigns, people reached and the pooled lift over the holdout. A campaigns table (product, status with days left, audience, reach in people, adds, incremental lift or "Collecting · needs 10 per group"; impressions, clicks, CTR, holdout and dates optional; `?all=1` adds other workspaces) whose rows open the campaign's own page (`/retail/campaigns/:id`, below; the older `?campaign=<id>` link redirects there). Then "Ready to promote" (eligible products with Promote; "Show not eligible yet" lists the api's reasons). `?new=<product_id>` (from Promote anywhere) opens the create sheet prefilled: product, audience (only the goals and skin types the evidence covers, narrow-only, with a matching-profiles range from the cohort), 7/14/30 days, holdout 10-50% (default 20%), and the exact Sponsored card with "Why am I seeing this?". Reach is unique people shown the card (each person once); impressions count each person once a day. "End campaign…" stops a campaign now and for good (it cannot be resumed; its results stay; one that ended before both groups had 10 people reads "Not measured", and the pipeline's Promoted step then offers "Promote again"); Delete removes it and its stats. The header's "people reached" sums each campaign's people, so a person two campaigns reached counts in both.
- **Campaign page** (`/retail/campaigns/:id`, `sections/CampaignDashboard.tsx`, no new endpoint: it reads the campaign from `GET /v1/retailer/campaigns`, which carries `stats`): breadcrumb, packshot, status, "Day 3 of 14", dates and Product evidence / Pause or Resume / End… / Delete… (End and Delete confirm in a bar under the header; `?confirm=end|delete` is how the table's menu lands there). Only three figures are numbers: people reached, added to routine, lift over the holdout (or "Needs 10 people in each group"). Everything else is read as an insight. The hero opens with a one-sentence answer (`campaignInsight`: "The card is making people add this product", "Too early to say whether the card changes anything", or what is happening while the holdout is still collecting; a verdict only once both groups have 10 people, and "making people add" only when the lift's 95% interval stays above zero) over the Retail design's stream chart of the running totals by day (card shown, clicked through, added to routine as nested bands growing from a hairline to full width, a glow under the outer band, three tick lines with the running total above and the day below; band width is the running total, so the bands nest as the totals do). Beside it, "With the card, without it": 100 dots per group, filled = added the product, and one sentence for the extra people it caused (lift x the people who could see the card, with its 95% interval; "isn't clear yet" when the range reaches zero). Below: "Where it works", the design's radial blob chart (one axis per goal, skin type or skin tone, whichever has the most groups of 10+ people; a layer for the people using the product and one for non-users with the same goals, both against the highest rate, the people measured in dotted digits at the centre; no shape is drawn under three groups) with the proof tier and the product's own record in a sentence, and "Who it reaches" (goals and skin types as chips, the card as a shopper sees it, the holdout share). Willow has no checkout and no sales feed, so "buying" is **adds to a routine** and the page says it does not show store sales or channels; delivery is never split by goal or skin type. Refreshes every 30 s; an unknown or someone else's id reads "Campaign not found".
- **Explore** (`/retail/explore`): a query builder over the product rows: measure (visible change rate, lift vs non-users, people measured, still using at week 8, weeks to first change, share of routines), group by (product, brand, category, price tier, Proof status, goal), filters, minimum people, sort and limit, five presets; a scale-true bar chart (your brand in leaf, intervals, non-user ticks) and a table, with Copy link and CSV. Groups are people-weighted (Σ value·n / Σ n; lift = weighted rate minus weighted non-user rate); groups with nothing left are dropped, never shown as 0.

**AutoCampaign** (Campaigns → AutoCampaign; api `autopilot.py`, `autopilot_agent.py`, `routes/autopilot_routes.py`; web `sections/promote/AutoCampaigns.tsx`, `RuleEditor.tsx`, helpers `autopilot.ts`, tests `api/tests/test_autopilot*.py`, `web/tests/autopilot.test.ts`). Standing rules that watch the evidence: scope (category or products), trigger (`proven`, `new_segment`, `rising_demand`, `unmet_need`), conditions (in store, start/stop dates, monthly cap), action (days, holdout, max running) and level (`suggest` = propose for approval, `auto` = start and notify, `autopilot` = also pause, extend, narrow and end what it started). A rule can be typed in plain words (Gemini reads it; a regex fallback). Each check runs every enabled rule as a Gemini tool-calling episode inside the same guardrails as a manual campaign (code refuses any action outside them and any reason quoting a number the tools didn't return); without Gemini the fixed logic runs and the feed says so. Proposals are approved, dismissed or undone in the feed and appear as cards on Home. Rules and approvals need an analyst or admin membership (403 `not_a_member`, shown as "AutoCampaign needs an analyst or admin" with "Choose how to explore"). Checks run when the page opens on a stale run or on "Run check now"; the nightly `/v1/internal/autopilot` job is not configured in `infra/` yet.

**Shelf check** (Campaigns → Shelf check, retailer only; api `shelf.py`, `shelf_agent.py`, `routes/shelf_routes.py`; web `sections/market/ShelfResults.tsx`, tests `api/tests/test_shelf.py`, `web/tests/shelf.test.ts`). Per category, each measured product's share of use (or of the uploaded facings/stock) against its share of the people with a visible change; four flag kinds (taking space, under-shelved, stocked but little used, used but under-stocked), fixed in code with a 5-point rule and re-ordered and worded by a Gemini agent in the background (cached 6 hours). The stock CSV (up to 500 rows; `product_id` or `product` plus `facings`/`stock_units`) is stored per person at `shelf/{uid}`; the Range upload forwards its facings. Flags feed Home's cards 6 and 7.

**Competitor names for brands** (research 06 §4.2). A retailer-level policy (Settings → Data sharing, off by default). Off: a brand organisation sees other brands as "Brand A", "Brand B"… (letters from the catalog's brands in code-point order, the same in the web, `masking.ts`, and the api, `retailer_ask.scope_for`) and their products as "A Brand C product"; another brand's product page, Explore, the whole catalog and Compare links that name another brand's product show "hidden by the retailer's policy". Every file that leaves Proof (CSV, evidence pack, brief, digest, copied text) passes through the same mask while it is on (`ui/format.ts` `setExportFilter`, set by the shell), so no export path can carry a competitor's name. **Ask** inside an organisation goes to `POST /v1/proof/orgs/{org}/ask` (viewer+): a brand asks about its own brand ("we" = it); with names off Gemini sees only the aliases, and an answer, follow-up or chart label that names another brand or one of its products is rejected (asked again once, then "I could not answer without naming other brands…"). The retailer may focus Ask on the brand it is viewing and always sees names. The public `POST /v1/retailer/ask` is unchanged.

## AI in Willow Proof (Oct 18)
Proof answers "what works"; a brand manager's next question is "so what do I do, and what may I say?". Gemini is used where it saves them real work, always on the facts already on the page and never for a number (`api/app/proof_ai.py`, `docs/GEMINI_SPEC.md` §8):
- **Next steps** on the product page (`GET /v1/retailer/products/{id}/actions`): the api lists every action the data supports, each with its figures and a link into Proof: promote to the skin type where it works best (opens the campaign flow prefilled), tell buyers when results show (many users changed but didn't say it works: an early-quit risk), answer the most-chosen "not liked" feel tag (heavy: position for dry skin or night; stings: say how to start; drying: pair with a moisturiser), keep users past week 8 (and where switchers go), get a Promising product to Proven, don't target a skin type where it does worse than non-users, explain layering for a common routine clash, bundle with a product its users already pair it with, draft claims. Gemini picks the three most valuable for this product and says why in a sentence; without it, a fixed priority.
- **Claim drafts** on the product page (`POST /v1/retailer/products/{id}/claims`): copy for the pack, the brand's product page, a retailer listing or a social caption, written only from the product's evidence statements and, labelled, what users said. Each draft cites the statements it rests on; numbers, overclaims a regulator would challenge ("clinically proven", "cures", condition names, "everyone"), channel length and self-reported wording are checked before it is shown; the statements themselves when nothing passes. Proven and Promising products only.
- **Ask** also knows what users said about each product (self-reported share and n, 10+ only), so "which products do people say are working, and is it measured?" has an answer.
Why these and not a generic chatbot: drafting compliant claims and deciding where to spend promotion are the two jobs a brand does with evidence like this, and both go wrong without grounding (overclaims, promoting to the wrong skin type). Every output can be traced back to a number on the page.
